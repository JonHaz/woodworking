# Red oak stock — Lowe's

Purchasable red oak (S4S — surfaced four sides) sizes Jon can buy locally. This
is a **sourcing reference for what can be bought**; it is *not* on-hand
inventory — for stock actually in the shop see
[materials on hand](/shop/materials-on-hand.md), and always allocate on-hand
stock before adding to a shopping list.

## How to read these

Sizes are quoted **nominal** (thickness × width), the way the store lists them.
Real S4S boards are surfaced smaller than nominal, so **cut-list math must use
the actual dimensions** below, never the nominal. Length is sold at the nominal
foot.

## Common measurement (thickness × width)

| Nominal T × W | Typical S4S actual (in) |
|---|---|
| 1 × 2 | 0.75 × 1.5 |
| 1 × 3 | 0.75 × 2.5 |
| **1 × 4** | **0.75 × 3.5** ← confirmed (ReliaBilt) |
| 1 × 6 | 0.75 × 5.5 |
| 1 × 8 | 0.75 × 7.25 |
| 1 × 12 | 0.75 × 11.25 |
| 1/4 × 2 | 0.25 × 1.5 |
| 1/4 × 3 | 0.25 × 2.5 |
| 1/4 × 4 | 0.25 × 3.5 |
| 1/4 × 6 | 0.25 × 5.5 |
| 1/2 × 2 | 0.5 × 1.5 |
| 1/2 × 3 | 0.5 × 2.5 |
| 1/2 × 4 | 0.5 × 3.5 |
| 1/2 × 6 | 0.5 × 5.5 |
| 2 × 2 | 1.5 × 1.5 |
| 2 × 8 | 1.5 × 7.25 |
| 3 × 3 | 2.5 × 2.5 |

Actual-size conventions used above: **thickness** 1 in → 0.75, 2 in → 1.5,
3 in → 2.5; the 1/4 in and 1/2 in boards run at (or just under) their nominal
thickness. **Width** 2 → 1.5, 3 → 2.5, 4 → 3.5, 6 → 5.5, 8 → 7.25, 12 → 11.25.

> ⚠ **Measure the real board before doing cut-list math.** Hardwood S4S
> surfacing varies between runs and suppliers — the thin (1/4 in, 1/2 in) and
> wide (8 in, 12 in) boards are the most likely to differ from the table. Only
> the 1×4 = 0.75 × 3.5 anchor is confirmed measured; treat the rest as typical
> until checked with a tape.

## Notes for planning

- Boards are solid red oak, so unlike sheet goods they can be **edge-glued** into
  wider panels and **ripped/crosscut** freely (Jon's [confirmed tools](/shop/tools.md)
  include a table saw, track saw, miter saw, and router).
- Allow the standard **~1/8 in kerf per cut** in yield math (see
  [lessons learned](/methods/lessons-learned.md) #3).
- Widths above 4 in are single boards; for a wider top, glue up from narrower
  boards to keep cost down and reduce cupping.

A 4-foot module that lines up with the 3-foot bench. Same depth, same height, same recessed base logic — only wider, with a fourth cubby. Build one, set it beside a finished 3-footer to confirm the run is flush, then batch the rest.

Works
alongside

Depth stays 17 in and height stays 18.5 in, both with zero overhang — so a 3-ft and 4-ft sit dead flush, fronts and backs aligned, tops forming one continuous seat. Base is inset 1.5 in just like the 3-ft. Openings are 11-1/16 in vs the 3-ft's 11 in: the same cubby rhythm to the eye.

Per bench: about one ¾ plywood sheet + roughly 1½ eight-foot 2×4s, plus glue, 1¼–2 in screws, and rubber pads (edges filled and sanded — no banding). The extra divider and the wider base are the only differences from the 3-ft.

Build sequence

01

Cut & label every part

Break the sheet down per Fig. 4, then crosscut to size. Tape a name on each piece — a 4-ft bench has thirteen plywood parts and three near-identical dividers.

CHECK: sides 14.25 × 17 · shelf 46.5 × 17 · dividers ×3 @ 13.5 × 16.25
02

Fill & sand the show edges

Fill and sand the front edges of both sides, all three dividers, and the shelf, plus all four edges of the top. Do it flat, before assembly. (No edge banding — the shop preference; iron-on banding is the alternative only if you want a natural, unpainted finish.)

03

Shelf between the sides

Glue-and-screw the 46.5-in shelf between the two sides, flush at the bottom and front. This box references everything else.

04

Rear bottom stretcher

Set the lower 46.5 × 3 stretcher on the shelf at the back, flush with the rear edges of the sides. Glue and screw through the sides. It stiffens the box and leaves a 2¼-in lip at the open back.

05

Set the three dividers — use a spacer block

Don't chase fractions. Cut one scrap spacer exactly 11-1/16 in wide. Stand the first divider tight against the spacer off the left inside face, fasten, move the spacer to the next gap, repeat. Four identical openings, no cumulative error.

06

Rear top stretcher

Fit the upper 46.5 × 3 stretcher between the sides at the top rear, flush with the back and the top edges. Screw through the sides and back into each divider edge. The rear plane is now tied together — the racking control on a backless bench, and it matters more on the wider span.

The carcass should feel rigid before the top goes on. Wider bench, so check squareness at both ends and the middle.
07

Build the 2×4 base

Ladder frame: two 45-in rails front and back, four 11-in rungs between them (two ends + two center supports — the longer rails need the extra support). All 2×4 on edge, glued and screwed. Footprint 45 × 14.

08

Marry base to carcass

Flip the carcass, center the base on the shelf underside (1.5 in showing all around), and screw down through the shelf into the rails. Countersink so nothing snags in the cubbies.

09

Fasten the top

Set the 48 × 17 top flush all around and secure with glue plus screws — or dowels — driven up from inside — through both sides, all three dividers, and the top stretcher. The top is the lifting handle; don't trust brads or glue alone.

Heavier top on this size — mechanical fasteners into it are a safety item, not a nicety.
10

Pads, finish, dry-fit

Add four to six rubber pads under the base. Sand, prime, paint or stain. Then set the finished 4-ft next to a 3-ft: the fronts, backs, and tops should line up with no gap. Dry-stack two 4-footers and confirm the nest before batching more.

Stacking, mixing & safety

▲

Stack like-with-like

  • 4-on-4 nests exactly like 3-on-3: the 45-in base drops inside the 48-in top and must travel 1.5 in to clear. Safe for static storage with pads.
  • Mixed 3-and-4 stacks work but lose the self-centering recess — a narrower base on a wider top can wander before it hits an edge. If you must mix, put the wider bench on the bottom and strap the stack.
  • Heights are unchanged: 2 high = 37 in, 3 high = 55.5 in (strap around kids), 6 high = 111 in (don't, unless wall-strapped).

At ~60–65 lb, the 4-ft is a firm two-person lift every time. Grab under the top at the open back, where the rear stretcher makes a natural handhold — no cutouts, so the sides stay flush for the run.

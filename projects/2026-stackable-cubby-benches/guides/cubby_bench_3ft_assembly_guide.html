3-Foot Cubby Bench

Starts fromcut, edge-finished parts
Finished36 × 17 × 18.5 in
Time~2–3 hr + glue cure

Assembly only — this assumes every part is already cut to the sizes in the cut diagrams and the show edges are filled and sanded. Work on a flat surface. Pre-drill and countersink all screw holes so the plywood doesn't split at edges.

Assembly sequence

01

Lay out & dry-fit

Set out all parts. Stand the two sides on edge and slot the bottom shelf between them — it should measure 36 in across the outside. Mark screw lines, then pre-drill and countersink. A quick dry-fit now catches a mis-cut before glue is involved.

Keep the better face of each panel facing out/into the cubbies. Mark the show faces with tape so they stay consistent.
02

Build the base ladder

Lay the two 33-in rails parallel, drop in the three 11-in rungs (two at the ends, one centered), all 2×4 on edge. Glue and drive 3-in screws through the rails into each rung end. Check it's square before the glue sets.

03

Shelf between the sides

Stand the sides up, run a bead of glue on the shelf ends, and set the 34.5-in shelf between them — flush at the bottom and flush at the front. Screw through the outside of each side into the shelf edge. This box references everything after it.

04

Rear bottom stretcher

Set one 34.5 × 3 stretcher on the shelf at the back, standing on edge, flush with the rear edges of the sides. Glue and screw through the sides into its ends. It stiffens the box and leaves a 2¼-in lip that keeps items from sliding out the open back.

05

Set the two dividers

Cut a scrap spacer 11 in wide. Butt it against the left inside face, stand the first divider tight to it, and fasten down through the shelf. Move the spacer to the other side of that divider and repeat for the second. Three equal 11-in cubbies, no fractions.

06

Rear top stretcher

Fit the second 34.5 × 3 stretcher between the sides at the top rear, flush with the back and with the top edges of the sides. Screw through the sides and back into each divider's rear edge. The whole back plane is now tied together — this is what stops the backless bench from racking.

07

Marry the base to the carcass

Turn the carcass upside down. Center the base ladder on the underside of the shelf so 1.5 in shows on all four sides, then screw down through the shelf into the rails. Countersink so the heads sit below the cubby floor.

08

Fasten the top

Turn the bench upright. Run glue along the top edges of the sides, dividers, and top stretcher, set the 36 × 17 top on flush all the way around, and drive screws up from inside into it — through each side, both dividers, and the top stretcher. Dowels give the cleanest look here; straight screws from inside work too.

Don't rely on brads or glue alone for the top. A top that lets go mid-stack is how a bench falls.
09

Pads, finish & check

Add four to six rubber pads to the base underside. Confirm the bench sits without a wobble and reads square (equal diagonals across the top). Fill and sand the exposed edges, then prime and paint. Set it beside a finished bench to confirm the fronts and tops line up flush.

SQUARE CHECK: measure corner-to-corner both ways across the top — equal diagonals means it's square. Nudge and clamp before the glue fully cures if they differ.

Finished bench

Two-person lift (~45–50 lb). Carry by gripping under the top at the open back. Don't stack more than 3 high unless strapped to a wall.

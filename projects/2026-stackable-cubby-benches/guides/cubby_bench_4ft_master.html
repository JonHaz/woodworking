At a glance

Everything for the four 4-foot cubby benches in one document — shopping list, both cut diagrams, and the full ten-step build. The 4-ft is the 3-ft bench made wider: same 17-in depth, same 18.5-in height, same recessed base, with a fourth cubby. Build one, set it beside a finished 3-footer to confirm the run is flush, then batch the other three. One 4×8 plywood sheet makes exactly one bench; six new 8-ft 2×4s make all four bases.

What you're building

Build & assembly sequence

Ten unified steps, starting from cut, edge-finished parts. Work on a flat surface. Pre-drill and countersink every screw hole so the plywood doesn't split at edges. Reinforced joints use dowels (Jon's dowel jig — no pocket-hole jig); straight screws driven from inside are the primary fastener.

  • Tools: drill/driver, countersink bit, square, tape, clamps, spacer block (11-1/16 in), pencil, dowel jig.

01

Cut, label & dry-fit

Break each sheet down per Fig. 1, then crosscut to size and tape a name on every piece — a 4-ft bench has thirteen plywood parts and three near-identical dividers. Stand the two sides on edge and slot the shelf between them; the outside should measure 48 in. A dry-fit now catches a mis-cut before glue is involved.

CHECK: sides 14.25 × 17 · shelf 46.5 × 17 · dividers ×3 @ 13.5 × 16.25 · stretchers ×2 @ 46.5 × 3. Keep the better face of each panel toward the cubbies; mark show faces with tape.
02

Fill & sand the show edges

Fill and sand the front edges of both sides, all three dividers, and the shelf, plus all four edges of the top. Do it flat, before assembly. (No edge banding — the shop preference; iron-on banding is the alternative only if you want a natural, unpainted finish.)

03

Shelf between the sides

Run a bead of glue on the shelf ends and set the 46.5-in shelf between the two sides — flush at the bottom and flush at the front. Screw through the outside of each side into the shelf edge. This box references everything after it.

04

Rear bottom stretcher

Set one 46.5 × 3 stretcher on the shelf at the back, standing on edge, flush with the rear edges of the sides. Glue and screw through the sides into its ends. It stiffens the box and leaves a 2¼-in lip that keeps items from sliding out the open back.

05

Set the three dividers — use a spacer block

Don't chase fractions. Cut one scrap spacer exactly 11-1/16 in wide. Stand the first divider tight against the spacer off the left inside face, fasten, move the spacer to the next gap, repeat. Four identical openings, no cumulative error.

06

Rear top stretcher

Fit the upper 46.5 × 3 stretcher between the sides at the top rear, flush with the back and the top edges. Screw through the sides and back into each divider edge. The rear plane is now tied together — the racking control on a backless bench, and it matters more on the wider span.

SQUARE CHECK: with the carcass rigid, measure corner-to-corner both ways across the open face — equal diagonals means square. On this wider bench, check at both ends and the middle; nudge and clamp before the glue sets.
07

Build the 2×4 base

Ladder frame: two 45-in rails front and back, four 11-in rungs between them (two ends + two center supports — the longer rails need the extra support). All 2×4 on edge, glued and driven with 3-in screws through the rails into each rung end. Check it's square before the glue sets. Footprint 45 × 14 (see Fig. 3).

08

Marry base to carcass

Flip the carcass, center the base on the shelf underside so 1.5 in shows on all four sides, and screw down through the shelf into the rails. Countersink so nothing snags in the cubbies.

09

Fasten the top

Turn the bench upright. Run glue along the top edges of both sides, all three dividers, and the top stretcher, set the 48 × 17 top on flush all around, and drive fasteners up from inside into it — through each side, all three dividers, and the top stretcher. Dowels give the cleanest look here; straight screws from inside work too. The top is the lifting handle; don't trust brads or glue alone.

Heavier top on this size — mechanical fasteners into it are a safety item, not a nicety. A top that lets go mid-stack is how a bench falls.
10

Pads, finish & dry-fit

Add four to six rubber pads under the base. Confirm the bench sits without a wobble and reads square (equal diagonals across the top). Sand, prime, paint or stain. Then set the finished 4-ft next to a 3-ft: the fronts, backs, and tops should line up with no gap. Dry-stack two 4-footers and confirm the nest before batching more.

FINAL SQUARE CHECK: equal top diagonals + no rock on a flat floor = ready to batch the remaining three.

Stacking, mixing & safety

▲

Stack like-with-like

  • 4-on-4 nests exactly like 3-on-3: the 45-in base drops inside the 48-in top and must travel 1.5 in to clear. Safe for static storage with pads.
  • Mixed 3-and-4 stacks work but lose the self-centering recess — a narrower base on a wider top can wander before it hits an edge. If you must mix, put the wider bench on the bottom and strap the stack.
  • Heights are unchanged from the 3-ft: 2 high = 37 in, 3 high = 55.5 in (strap around kids), 6 high = 111 in (don't, unless wall-strapped).

At ~60–65 lb, the 4-ft is a firm two-person lift every time. Grab under the top at the open back, where the rear stretcher makes a natural handhold — no cutouts, so the sides stay flush for the run.

Fasten every top with screws (or dowels) from inside — it is the lifting handle. Don't stack more than 3 high unless wall-strapped.

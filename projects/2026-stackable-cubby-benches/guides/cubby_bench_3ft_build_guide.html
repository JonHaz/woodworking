For all six: 4 sheets of ¾ plywood + 3 new eight-foot 2×4s + 8 on-hand 54-1/8 offcuts, plus glue, 1¼–2 in screws, and rubber pads. Fill and sand the show edges — no banding.

Build sequence

01

Cut & label every part

Break the sheets down per Fig. 3, then crosscut to the sizes in the cut list. Write the part name on painter's tape on each piece — twelve near-identical panels get confusing fast.

CHECK: sides 14.25 × 17 · shelf 34.5 × 17 · dividers 13.5 × 16.25
02

Fill & sand the show edges

Fill and sand the front edges of both sides, both dividers, and the shelf, plus all four edges of the top. Do it now, while everything is flat and easy to work. (No edge banding — the shop preference; if you want a natural, unpainted finish instead, iron-on banding is the alternative, applied now while flat.)

03

Shelf between the sides

Stand the two sides up and glue-and-screw the bottom shelf between them, flush at the bottom and flush at the front. This is the box that everything else references.

04

Rear bottom stretcher

Set the lower 34.5 × 3 stretcher on the shelf at the very back, flush with the rear edges of the sides. Glue and screw through the sides into its ends. It stiffens the box and leaves a 2¼-in lip that keeps loose items from sliding out the open back.

05

Set the two dividers

Best method: cut a scrap 11-in spacer block and step it across from the left inside face — set the first divider against it, then move the spacer past that divider and set the second against it. (By measurement the divider left faces land at 11 in and 22.75 in — 11 + 0.75 divider + 11 — not 24.) Stand each divider on the shelf, back edge butted to the bottom stretcher, and fasten down through the shelf (or pocket-screw from below). Square each one as you go.

06

Rear top stretcher

Fit the upper 34.5 × 3 stretcher between the sides at the top rear, flush with the back and with the top edges of the sides. Screw through the sides into its ends and back into the divider edges. Now the whole rear plane is tied together — this is what fights racking on a backless bench.

The carcass should feel rigid before the top goes on. If it still racks, check that both stretchers are seated flush at the back.
07

Build the 2×4 base

Assemble a flat ladder: two 33-in rails front and back, two 11-in rails at the ends, one 11-in support in the middle — all 2×4 on edge, glued and screwed. Finished footprint is 33 × 14.

08

Marry base to carcass

Flip the carcass upside down, center the base on the underside of the shelf (1.5 in showing on all four sides), and screw down through the shelf into the rails. Countersink so nothing snags inside the cubbies.

09

Fasten the top

Set the top on, flush all the way around, and secure it with glue plus pocket screws driven up from inside — through the sides, both dividers, and the top stretcher. The top gets grabbed on every lift and stack, so don't trust brads or glue alone.

Mechanical fasteners into the top are a safety item, not a nicety — a top that pops loose mid-stack is how benches fall.
10

Pads, then finish

Add four to six rubber pads to the underside of the base. Sand, prime, and paint or stain. Then dry-stack two benches and confirm the nest feels secure before you commit to building the other five.

Stacking & safety

▲

How high, and when to strap

  • 2 high · 37 in — fine anywhere. Or nest two base-to-base with a moving blanket between so nothing rests on a finished top.
  • 3 high · 55.5 in — fine; strap the stack if kids are around.
  • 6 high · 111 in — don't, unless it's strapped to a wall. Too tall and tippy.

Recommended storage: two stacks of three, or three stacks of two. Move benches two people at a time — grab under the top at the open back, where the rear stretcher makes a natural handhold, so no cutouts are needed and the sides stay flush.

At a glance

Six identical open-cubby benches. Each is 36 × 17 × 18.5 in with three equal 11-in cubbies, a flush top with zero overhang, and a recessed 2×4 base that lets one bench nest on top of another. Every panel is ¾-in plywood with filled-and-sanded show edges — no banding — joined with glue and screws (butt joints, no face frame); the base is 2×4 on edge.

Build one bench start to finish, dry-fit the stack, then batch the other five. The first bench proves the cuts and the nest before you commit material to the remaining carcasses. This one document carries the shopping list, both cut diagrams, the elevations, the full build-and-assembly sequence, and the stacking rules.

What you're building

Build & assembly sequence

Ten steps from labeled parts to a finished, dry-fit bench. Work on a flat surface; pre-drill and countersink every screw so the plywood doesn't split at edges. Plan on ~2–3 hr plus glue cure per bench.

Hardware · one bench
ItemApprox
Wood screws, 1¼–2 in~34
Construction screws, 3 in (base)~12
Wood glue—
Rubber pads4–6
  • Tools: drill/driver, countersink bit, square, tape, clamps, spacer block (11 in), pencil.
  • Optional: dowels for a cleaner, screw-free-looking top attachment — owner has a dowel jig (no pocket-hole jig needed).
01

Cut, label & dry-fit

Break the sheets down per Figs. 1–3, then crosscut to the sizes in the cut list. Write the part name on painter's tape on each piece — twelve near-identical panels get confusing fast. Stand the two sides on edge and slot a shelf between them: it should measure 36 in across the outside. Mark screw lines, pre-drill, and countersink. A quick dry-fit now catches a mis-cut before glue is involved.

CHECK: sides 14.25 × 17 · shelf 34.5 × 17 · dividers 13.5 × 16.25
Keep the better face of each panel facing out / into the cubbies. Mark the show faces with tape so they stay consistent.
02

Fill & sand the show edges

Fill and sand the front edges of both sides, both dividers, and the shelf, plus all four edges of the top. Do it now, while everything is flat and easy to work. (No edge banding — the shop preference; if you want a natural, unpainted finish instead, iron-on banding is the alternative, applied now while flat.)

03

Shelf between the sides

Stand the two sides up, run a bead of glue on the shelf ends, and glue-and-screw the bottom shelf between them — flush at the bottom and flush at the front. Drive screws through the outside of each side into the shelf edge. This is the box that everything else references.

04

Rear bottom stretcher

Set the lower 34.5 × 3 stretcher on the shelf at the very back, standing on edge, flush with the rear edges of the sides. Glue and screw through the sides into its ends. It stiffens the box and leaves a 2¼-in lip that keeps loose items from sliding out the open back.

05

Set the two dividers

Best method: cut a scrap 11-in spacer block and step it across from the left inside face — set the first divider against it, then move the spacer past that divider and set the second against it. (By measurement the divider left faces land at 11 in and 22.75 in — 11 + 0.75 divider + 11 — not 24.) Stand each divider on the shelf, back edge butted to the bottom stretcher, and fasten down through the shelf (or dowel from below). Square each one as you go.

06

Rear top stretcher

Fit the upper 34.5 × 3 stretcher between the sides at the top rear, flush with the back and with the top edges of the sides. Screw through the sides into its ends and back into the divider edges. Now the whole rear plane is tied together — this is what fights racking on a backless bench.

The carcass should feel rigid before the top goes on. If it still racks, check that both stretchers are seated flush at the back.
07

Build the 2×4 base

Assemble a flat ladder: two 33-in rails front and back, three 11-in rungs (two at the ends, one centered) — all 2×4 on edge, glued with 3-in screws through the rails into each rung end. Check it's square before the glue sets. Finished footprint is 33 × 14. (Cut layout and the six-base tally live in the 2×4 base section above.)

08

Marry the base to the carcass

Flip the carcass upside down, center the base on the underside of the shelf so 1.5 in shows on all four sides, and screw down through the shelf into the rails. Countersink so the heads sit below the cubby floor and nothing snags inside the cubbies.

09

Fasten the top

Turn the bench upright. Run glue along the top edges of the sides, dividers, and top stretcher, set the 36 × 17 top on flush all the way around, and secure it with screws — or dowels — driven up from inside: through each side, both dividers, and the top stretcher. Dowels give the cleanest look; straight screws from inside are the primary method. The top gets grabbed on every lift and stack, so don't trust brads or glue alone.

Fasten the top from inside — it's a safety item, not a nicety. Don't rely on brads or glue alone: a top that lets go mid-stack is how a bench falls.
10

Pads, finish & check

Add four to six rubber pads to the underside of the base. Confirm the bench sits without a wobble and reads square (equal diagonals across the top). Sand, prime, and paint or stain. Then dry-stack two benches and set one beside a finished bench to confirm the nest feels secure and the fronts and tops line up flush — before you commit to building the other five.

SQUARE CHECK: measure corner-to-corner both ways across the top — equal diagonals means it's square. Nudge and clamp before the glue fully cures if they differ.
Two-person lift (~45–50 lb). Carry by gripping under the top at the open back, where the rear stretcher makes a natural handhold — no cutouts needed, sides stay flush.

Stacking & safety

▲

How high, and when to strap

  • 2 high · 37 in — fine anywhere. Or nest two base-to-base with a moving blanket between so nothing rests on a finished top.
  • 3 high · 55.5 in — fine; strap the stack if kids are around.
  • 6 high · 111 in — don't, unless it's strapped to a wall. Too tall and tippy.

Stack like with like — only nest same-size benches so the recessed base seats cleanly on the top below. Recommended storage: two stacks of three, or three stacks of two. Move benches two people at a time — grab under the top at the open back, where the rear stretcher makes a natural handhold, so no cutouts are needed and the sides stay flush.

Strap any stack 3 high or taller. A backless bench stack has no lateral tie beyond the nest, so a wall strap is the safety margin.

Build-order checklist

Rip & crosscut all plywood; label every piece with tape.
Cut 2×4 base parts; build 6 ladder bases (glue + 3-in screws).
Build 1 bench fully; dry-stack 2 & set side-by-side to confirm fit.
Batch-assemble the remaining 5 carcasses.
Fasten tops from inside; add pads; sand, prime, finish.
Fasten every top with screws (or dowels) from inside — it's the lifting handle. All benches are two-person lifts (~45–50 lb). Don't stack over 3 high unless wall-strapped.

# Stackable 3-Foot Modular Cubby Bench — Revision 3 (Corrected)

## Revision 3 corrections (2026-07-21)

Two shop decisions made after Rev 2 supersede the notes below:

1. **No edge banding.** Exposed plywood edges are **filled and sanded**, not
   iron-on banded — the shop's standing preference (supersedes Rev 2 change #1).
   Edge banding is only an optional alternative if you want a natural, unpainted
   finish.
2. **2×4 sourcing uses on-hand stock first.** Phase 1 consumes **8 on-hand
   54-1/8 in 2×4 offcuts plus 3 new eight-foot 2×4s** — not 7 new sticks
   (supersedes Rev 2 change #8). See `knowledge/shop/materials-on-hand.md`.

## What Changed From the Reviewed Draft

1. **Face frame removed.** Plywood edges are finished with iron-on edge banding (or filler if painting). This fixes the trim-fit conflict, widens cubby openings to a full 11 in, and removes the bottom lip. *(Superseded by Rev 3: edges are filled and sanded — no banding.)*
2. **Carcass deepened to 17 in.** With no face frame, the sides, shelf, and top all run the full 17 in depth, giving a true 36 × 17 footprint with zero overhang on all four sides.
3. **Bottom shelf corrected to 34.5 in wide** so it fits between the side panels (the draft's 36 in shelf could not physically fit).
4. **Dividers corrected to 13.5 in tall × 16.25 in deep** — they sit on the shelf and stop short of the rear stretchers, no notching required.
5. **Rear stretchers corrected to 34.5 in** and inset between the sides, flush with the back. The bottom stretcher stands on the shelf and doubles as a 2.25 in lip that keeps items from sliding out the open back.
6. **Base side rails corrected to 11 in** so the base actually finishes at 33 × 14 with a 1.5 in inset on all four sides (the draft's 14 in rails produced a full-depth base with no front/back recess).
7. **Locator blocks replaced** with rubber pads plus an optional strap; the draft's 1.5 × 1.5 × 3 blocks would have hung 3 in below the base.
8. **Material and lumber counts corrected:** 4 sheets of plywood and 7 eight-foot 2x4s (draft estimated 9–10). *(Superseded by Rev 3: 3 new 2x4s + 8 on-hand 54-1/8 offcuts.)*

Material decision: **3/4 in plywood, not MDF.** In plywood each bench weighs roughly 45–50 lb; in MDF closer to 70 lb, which is unreasonable for repeated lifting and stacking and holds screws poorly at the edges.

## Assembly Sequence

1. Cut all plywood parts; label them.
2. Fill and sand the front edges of sides, dividers, and shelf, and all four top edges (no edge banding). Do it before assembly — far easier flat. *(If you instead want a natural, unpainted finish, iron-on banding is the alternative; apply it now while flat.)*
3. Assemble the carcass: glue and screw the bottom shelf between the two sides, flush at the bottom and front.
4. Install the rear bottom stretcher on the shelf, flush with the back, glued and screwed through the sides.
5. Install the dividers at 11 in spacing: glue plus screws down through pre-drilled holes in the shelf, or dowels from below. Their back edges butt the bottom stretcher.
6. Install the rear top stretcher between the sides, flush with the back and with the top edges of the sides; screw through the sides and into the divider back edges. This ties the whole back plane together against racking.
7. Build the 2x4 base as a ladder frame (glue + 3 in construction screws). Center it under the shelf (1.5 in inset all around) and screw down through the shelf into the rails, countersunk inside the cubbies.
8. Attach the top with glue plus screws — or dowels — driven up from inside the carcass (through sides, dividers, and top stretcher). The top gets grabbed during every lift and stack, so do not rely on brads or glue alone.
9. Add rubber pads to the base underside. Sand, prime, paint or stain.

Joinery: butt joints with glue and screws (or dowels) are sufficient as dimensioned. If you prefer dados/rabbets for the shelf and dividers, add the dado depth to the captured part's dimension — the cut list above assumes butt joints.

## Structure and Safety Summary

- **Adult seating:** the 3/4 in top has supports every ~11 in (sides plus two dividers), which comfortably handles two adults. The dividers must be fastened to both shelf and top so they act as columns.
- **Racking:** the glued top and shelf act as diaphragms, and the two rear stretchers stiffen the open back. No full back panel needed for seating use.
- **Stacking:** the recessed base means an upper bench must slide 1.5 in before its base clears the edge of the bench below — combined with rubber pads on a smooth painted top, that is adequate for static storage. For a 2-high pair you can also stack top-to-top with a moving blanket between; the bases then face out and nothing rests on a finished surface.
- **Stack heights:** 2 high = 37 in (fine anywhere), 3 high = 55.5 in (fine, strap it if children are around), 6 high = 111 in (do not, unless strapped to a wall).
- **Handling:** at ~45–50 lb, one strong person can move a bench, but two-person lifts are the norm. Grip under the top at the open back (the top stretcher makes a natural handhold) — no cutouts needed, so the sides stay flush.

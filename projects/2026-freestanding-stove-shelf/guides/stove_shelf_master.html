At a glance

A liftable red-oak shelf that bridges the gap behind a 30-in electric range, adding display space above the cooktop without touching the wall or the counter. It simply rests on two legs — no wall cleats, no counter fasteners, no visible screws anywhere. Concealed 3/8-in dowels and glue hold the whole thing together; lift it by the top and it comes away as one ~7.5-lb piece. A hidden 3/8-in setback riser under each leg gives the top a real shadow reveal so it reads as floating, and a raised back fence stops jars from marching off the rear edge.

Dry-fit before you glue. This is a single build, not a batch — but the joinery is unforgiving (glue-only, no screws to fall back on), so the dry-fit and square check in Step 6 is the point of no return. Read the weight & stability section before you finish and place it: a tall, shallow base like this one is more tip-prone than it looks, and the fix is cheap (magnets + load discipline + feet).

Dowel locations & spacing

Every joint in this build is glue + concealed 3/8-in fluted dowels — there are no screws anywhere. The leg→top joint is the load-bearing one: 3 dowels per leg, run from the leg's end grain, through the pre-drilled riser, into long grain on the shelf underside.

STRETCHER & FENCE DOWELS: 2 per end into the leg inner faces (stretcher); 3–4 alignment dowels for the fence into the shelf's back edge and for the top's own edge-glue seam — a properly jointed long-grain edge joint carries that load on its own, the dowels just keep the boards coplanar during glue-up.

Floating-top reveal detail

The stretch-goal detail: a hidden 0.375-in setback riser between each leg top and the shelf underside, inset 0.5 in from the leg's front and back faces. The result is a 3/8-in shadow line on both visible faces of the leg — the top reads as if it's floating.

Build & assembly sequence

Eleven steps from milled parts to a finished, glued shelf. Because every joint is glue-only (no screws to lean on), the dry-fit in Step 6 is the checkpoint — don't glue past it until the opening and the square check both pass.

  • Tools: table saw or track saw (rip top to 6.75, rip fence/stretcher strips), miter saw (crosscuts), dowel jig, router with 1/8-in roundover bit, bar/pipe clamps, orbital sander (220–400).
  • Scrap spacer blocks: one 11-in block for the divider-style alignment habit, one stepped block for the leg's 0.875-in dowel spacing (Fig. 8).
01

Mill & label

Crosscut every part to length per the cut list (Fig. 6). Label each piece with painter's tape — several parts are close in size. Select the two best-matched boards for the top; book-match the grain if you can, since the edge-glue seam will be the most visible line on the piece.

CHECK: 2 top boards 31×3.5×0.75 · 2 legs 11.125×3.5×0.75 · 1 fence/stretcher blank 31×1.5×0.75 (before the 29.5 crosscut) · 2 risers 2.5×0.75×0.375
02

Edge-glue the top

Joint the mating edges of the two top boards true and square. Add 3–4 alignment dowels (they keep the boards coplanar during clamp-up — the edge joint itself carries the load), spread glue, and clamp the pair into a 7.0-in-wide panel. Use cauls across the seam to keep it flat; wipe squeeze-out before it skins over.

03

Rip the top to 6.75 in

Once the glue has cured, rip one edge clean on the table saw or track saw down to the finished 6.75-in depth. This single rip also trues the panel's edge for the fence and stretcher to reference against later.

04

Rip the fence/stretcher strips

Rip the shared 31-in blank into two 1.5-in-wide strips (Fig. 7). Crosscut one of them to 29.5 in — that one's the rear stretcher; the other stays full length as the back fence.

See Fig. 7 for the rip layout — one blank, two strips, one crosscut.
05

Drill dowels on the jig

Jig-drill every joint before any glue goes on: leg tops (end grain, 3 per leg per Fig. 8's spacing), the matching holes in the shelf underside, straight through both risers, the legs' inner faces (for the stretcher), and the shelf's back edge plus the fence (3–4 alignment holes). Step the spacer block leg to leg so the three holes land at the same 0.875/1.75/2.625-in positions on both.

See Fig. 8 for the exact spacing and the leg → riser → shelf dowel path.
06

Dry-fit & check

Assemble the whole bridge dry — no glue — top, both leg/riser pairs, and the stretcher. Confirm the clear opening under the shelf measures 11.5 in and that the assembly is square before you commit any glue.

This is the checkpoint. There are no screws to fall back on if something's off — every joint downstream is glue-only. Fix it dry, not after the glue is on.
07

Glue legs + risers to the top

Glue and dowel both legs — through their risers — to the shelf underside, inset 0.75 in from the front, flush with the shelf's left and right ends. Clamp and check that the reveal gap reads even on both the front and back faces of each leg before the glue sets.

See Figs. 5 & 9 for the dowel directions and the reveal gap this joint creates.
08

Glue in the rear stretcher

Glue and dowel the 29.5-in stretcher between the legs' inner faces. This is what fights racking now that the legs are committed to the top — the bridge should feel notably stiffer once it's in and clamped.

09

Glue on the back fence

Glue and dowel the back fence along the shelf's back edge, full width. This is the last structural joint — the shelf is now a complete, liftable unit.

The shelf is the lifting handle from here on. The glued leg→top dowel joints carry the legs' full weight whenever you pick the piece up — lift with two hands under the top, and don't yank a single front corner.
10

Rout the roundover & sand

Rout the 1/8-in roundover on every exposed edge and corner — top faces and ends, leg outer edges, fence top — easing the reveal edges by hand. Sand the whole piece to 220.

11

Finish, feet & magnets

Apply the finish schedule below. Once cured, add the four clear silicone feet to the front and back extremes of each leg, and recess the rare-earth magnets low in the leg front faces so they pull to the range's magnetic black rear backguard (position them to the measured backguard height). The magnets go on last.

SQUARE & STABILITY CHECK: set the finished shelf in place and confirm it sits flat with no rock. Then read the weight & stability section below before you load it with anything.

TIPPING — the real weakness. The base is only 3.5 in deep (leg depth) while the shelf sits 11.5 in up: this piece is inherently tippy forward. The wall behind it arrests backward tip only.
  • Vertical load on the 3/4-in front overhang: tips at ~28 lb — not a concern.
  • Horizontal bump at shelf height: tips at only ~1.8 lb empty (~3 lb with jars kept to the back). This is the one to actively manage.

Anti-tip recommendations in order of effectiveness

1Rare-earth magnets to the range's magnetic black rear backguard (the raised black trim strip at the back of the cooktop — confirmed magnetic). Each leg's front face sits right against the range rear (leg depth 3.5 = rear gap 3.5), so recess magnets low in the leg front faces to pull directly onto the ferrous backguard — no keeper plate needed; position them to the measured backguard height (~lower 2 in of each leg). Tethers the shelf to the ~150 lb range in the tipping direction — the reliable primary anti-tip measure; releases when the shelf is lifted off.
2Load discipline — heavy jars to the back, over the legs; never on the front overhang.
3Silicone feet at the front and back extremes of each leg — friction stops the base kicking out (slide-then-tip) and maximizes the effective footprint.
4If magnets aren't viable, consider deepening the leg footprint (a discreet forward foot) — a geometric fix, at some cost to the clean look and the 3.5-in gap fit.

Finish schedule

Primary
General Finishes High Performance (Satin), water-based topcoat. Sand to 220; raise grain with a damp rag and knock back at 320; 3 thin coats, scuff-sand 320–400 between; cure hard before use. Grease- and moisture-resistant, wipes clean.
Alternative
Rubio Monocoat Pure (hardwax-oil), single coat. Natural matte look, easy spot-repair; re-oil occasionally near the cooktop.

Safety

FORWARD-TIP RISK ON A HOT COOKTOP — use the magnets + load discipline + feet above. This is the one real structural weakness of a tall, shallow-footed shelf; don't skip the mitigations.
THE SHELF IS THE LIFTING HANDLE — the glued leg→top dowel joints carry the legs when you lift the piece off. Lift with two hands under the top; don't yank a single front corner.
KEEP COMBUSTIBLES OFF THE SHELF directly over active burners — it clears the grates by only ~0.25 in.
